# Voice: LinkedIn — Ommelo company page

## What this file was built from, and how far to trust it

**CONFIDENCE: LOW. This is the weakest file in the set and it should be read as a
starting position, not a description.**

Every other voice file describes a person who exists and who was interviewed for five
rounds. This one describes a **company page that has never posted**, for a product
that has not launched, run by one person who has said out loud that he is learning
marketing as he goes: `[Q]`

> "I'm also experimenting how to market things. So it is it is something good learning for me"

So there is no corpus here at all. Not speech, not writing, not a single published
post. What follows is built from three things that are real: the interview, the rails
in `CLAUDE.md`, and the ordinary mechanics of how company pages behave on LinkedIn.

Tags, same as everywhere: `[Q]` backed by a printed quote of Alif's, `[S]` supported
by a stated fact, `[G]` a guess to be deleted on contact with evidence.

**Fix it the same way the others get fixed.** The first ten posts from this page become
its corpus, and this file gets rewritten from them.

## The one rule that matters most on this page

**Ommelo is a product with one person behind it, and the page must never pretend
otherwise.** `[S — Alif is the founder and there is no team]`

This is not a moral point, it is a practical one. A company page with fewer than a
hundred followers, writing "we are excited to announce", is transparently one person
in a costume, and the audience Alif wants (experience design leads, design managers)
reads that instantly because they work at companies that do it.

So:

- **"I" when a person is speaking**, which is most of the time. "I built this because"
  and not "we built this because".
- **"Ommelo" as the subject when the product is doing something.** "Ommelo reads a file
  and flags" rather than "we help you".
- **Never "we"** as a stand-in for one person. `[G, but held firmly]` The only honest
  "we" is one that includes actual other people, and there are none yet.
- **Never "the team"**, never "our users", never "our customers".

If a sentence would be a lie about how many people work on Ommelo, it does not go out,
regardless of how normal it sounds in company-page English.

## What this page is actually for

**Followers, and being a real place to land.** `[S — stated by Alif]`

Be honest about the mechanics: **a new company page does not grow by posting.** It grows
because Alif's personal profile sends people to it, because the product is worth
following, and because there is something to see when someone arrives. `[G]`

Which means the page's job is mostly to **not be empty and not be embarrassing** when a
design lead who read one of Alif's posts clicks through. Posting five times a week from
a company page with no audience is work with no destination.

**Two or three posts a week is the right shape here, and one good one is better than
three.** The engine is Alif's profile, not this page.

## Who is speaking

The person who built the thing, talking about the problem it came from.

The credibility on this page is **entirely borrowed from Alif's working life**, and that
is fine, it is the only credibility that exists yet. Six years in enterprise
consultancy, a design language system argument he keeps having, a review problem he hit
himself: `[Q]`

> "there was not enough time for reviewing all the work that the team had done... it was also very difficult to convince these clients of the decisions that I was making"

That sentence is the whole reason this page exists, and something close to it should be
findable on the page at all times.

## Tone

Plain, specific, unexcited. `[G]`

The same warmth as everywhere else, because it is the same person: `[Q]`

> "I will not curse anyone. I will not be negative. I am always nice, and I don't, uh, be too critiquing anybody."

But **less warmth than his personal profile, not more.** Warmth from a company page reads
as marketing warmth, which is a different and worse substance. Where his own profile can
be enthusiastic about other people's work, this page should mostly be describing what
the product does and what it does not do.

**Enthusiasm is allowed about the problem. Never about the product.** "Design reviews at
the end of a project are the worst part of the job" is a sentence this page can write.
"Ommelo is going to change how teams review design" is not.

The single strongest asset Alif has on this channel is that he says the unfinished part
out loud without being asked: `[Q]`

> "How the insights are drawn is something I'm still figuring out."

**That posture is worth more than any feature post.** Most company pages cannot do it.
Use it often.

## Machine-writing tells to avoid

Everything in `voice-linkedin-alif.md` applies. These are the company-page-specific ones,
and they matter more here because this is exactly the surface where they breed:

- "We're excited to announce", "thrilled to share", "proud to unveil".
- The founding-story post with no specific detail in it.
- "Introducing Ommelo." as a standalone line.
- Three adjectives before the product does anything.
- "Built for design teams who want to ship faster without sacrificing quality." A
  template, and read as one on sight.
- The problem-agitate-solve structure. Design leads have sat through the training deck
  that teaches it.
- A milestone post about a milestone that is not one. "100 followers!" "One month in!"
- Any post whose only content is that a post is being made.
- Reposting Alif's personal post verbatim from the company page. Everyone who follows
  both sees it twice and it makes both accounts look automated.
- The tips carousel. Especially the tips carousel.

## Usual post shape

`[G] — no evidence at all, this is a starting structure.`

1. The specific problem, from Alif's real working life, in one or two lines.
2. What that costs, concretely.
3. What Ommelo does about it, in plain words, or honestly, what it does not do yet.
4. An ending that stops.

Length: **100 to 200 words.** Shorter than his personal posts. A company page has less
licence to take up space.

## Formatting

- Line breaks between paragraphs. Paragraphs of one to three sentences.
- No bold, no headers. LinkedIn does not render markdown.
- **A product screenshot beats anything designed.** `[G]` This page has a real advantage
  here: Alif is a designer and the artifact is the thing.
- No hashtags in the body. At most two at the very bottom, and honestly none is fine.
- No links in the body. Reach penalty is real and it is worse on company pages.
  Put the link in the first comment.

## Punctuation habits

- **No em dashes.** `[S]` Same hard rule as every other file. Enforced in code.
- Full stops. Plain sentences.
- **No exclamation marks.** `[Q — none in any of his writing samples]` On a company page
  they read as launch-day voice.
- No ellipses.

## Emoji

`[G]` None in the post body. Company-page emoji is decoration standing in for content.
The rocket is the single most damaging character available on this platform.

## Named post formats

Of the seven, only three belong on this page:

**The Founder Note.** The main format here. What is being built, what is unfinished,
what was learned this week. **No metrics, no waitlist numbers, no revenue.** See the
rails.

**The Small Artifact.** One thing built or changed, shown plainly, with a screenshot.
This is the format that grows a design product's page, because the audience is designers
and they will look at the artifact.

**The Quiet Cost.** The problem Ommelo exists for, stated as a cost rather than a pitch.
The strongest top-of-funnel format on this page, because it is useful whether or not
anyone ever uses the product.

**The Room**, **The Correction**, **The Question I Keep Getting** and **The Disagreement**
all belong on Alif's personal profile instead. They are a person's formats. Run through
a company page they read as a person's post with the name filed off.

## Subjects

Effectively one: **Building Ommelo.**

**Enterprise UX reality** is allowed when it is the problem Ommelo addresses, stated as a
problem rather than a lead-in to the product.

**Design leadership and craft** and **Design and AI in practice** belong on Alif's own
profile. If this page starts posting general design opinion, it becomes a second personal
account with worse reach.

## Words this page would never use

Everything from `voice-linkedin-alif.md`, plus the whole company-page register:

"we're on a mission", "reimagining", "the future of design", "supercharge", "effortless",
"seamless", "end-to-end", "solution", "platform" as a self-description, "empowering
designers", "AI-powered" as the first thing said about it, "trusted by", "join us",
"stay tuned", "watch this space", "big things coming", "excited to share", "game-changer",
"10x", "revolutionise", "disrupt".

Also: **"our users"** and **"the team"**, for the reason at the top of this file.

## The rails, in the shape they bite here

The privacy rails in `CLAUDE.md` apply in full. Four of them do the most work on this
page specifically, so they are restated:

- **No user count, no waitlist number, no revenue, no funding, no valuation.** Not
  "hundreds of designers", not "our first users", not "growing fast". `[S]` The only
  exception is a number Alif has deliberately decided to make public, and that decision
  is his and is made before the draft, not during it.
- **No invented user, quote or testimonial.** Not paraphrased, not composited, not
  "a designer told me". `[S]`
- **No unreleased plans beyond what Alif has already said publicly.** No roadmap, no
  launch date until it is a date.
- **No client work, ever.** Not blurred, not redacted, not described closely enough to
  identify. Ommelo's page is not a place where IBM iX work appears. `[S]`

## Do

- Say what it does before why it matters.
- Show the artifact.
- Name what it does not do yet. This buys more than any feature claim.
- Keep the problem in Alif's own working life, not in a market.
- Post less than feels right.

## Do not

- Do not use an em dash.
- Do not say "we" about one person.
- Do not invent a number, a user or a testimonial.
- Do not repost Alif's personal post from here.
- Do not put a link in the body.
- Do not launch anything from this page without Alif's explicit go-ahead on the exact
  copy.
- Do not post to fill the slot. An empty week on a company page costs nothing.

## Comment voice

**The page should comment almost never.** `[G, but confident]`

A company page commenting on a design lead's post is an interruption from a brand. The
same comment from Alif's own profile is a person joining a conversation, and it is worth
many times more. **Comments are Alif's profile's job.** See `voice-linkedin-alif.md`.

The one exception: replying to comments on the page's own posts. Do that every time,
same day, in the first person, as the person who built it. Two to four sentences,
answering the actual question, including when the answer is that it does not do that yet.

## Message voice

The page should not send messages. `[G]`

If a conversation starts from a page post, it moves to Alif's own profile and follows
`voice-linkedin-alif.md`, which is evidence-backed: one line, no closing full stop, no
greeting or sign-off. `[Q]`

> "Sure let me know, we can meet another day"

**Never a cold pitch, and never a connection request with a pitch attached.** The design
partner conversations Alif wants come from someone reading something and getting in
touch, not from outbound. Outbound from a company page with no track record converts at
roughly nothing and costs the account.
