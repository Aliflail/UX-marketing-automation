# Voice: Product Hunt

## What this file was built from, and how far to trust it

**CONFIDENCE: LOW to MEDIUM. This file is a special case.**

Updated from the interview of 2026-08-10 (`data/corpus/interview.md`).

Tags: `[Q]` backed by a printed quote, `[S]` supported, `[G]` guess.

Two findings from the interview matter here more than anywhere else.

**He states unsolved problems out loud, unprompted.** `[Q]`

> "How the insights are drawn is something I'm still figuring out."

That is exactly the register a good launch post needs, and most founders cannot do it. Use it.

**He is learning marketing as he goes and says so.** `[Q]`

> "I'm also experimenting how to market things. So it is it is something good learning for me"

Which means: do not write launch copy that sounds like a marketer wrote it. The honest version is both truer and better received.

**The special case:** Product Hunt is not a publishing channel. It is an event venue. `[G]`

Alif listed it as somewhere he wants to publish `[S]`, but nobody publishes on Product Hunt in the way they publish on LinkedIn or X. You launch on it, once, per product, and the rest of the time you comment on other people's launches. Treating it as a fourth daily channel would produce activity with no purpose.

So this file is thinner than the others on purpose, and it covers two different things: how to comment there in the ordinary weeks, and how to write the Ommelo launch when that day comes.

There is no daily draft for this channel. DRIP should not queue Product Hunt rows unless a launch is actually being prepared.

## Who is speaking

A designer who built a thing, talking to other people who build things. `[S — founder of Ommelo]`

Product Hunt's audience is makers, early adopters and other founders. It is not the design-leads audience Alif said he wants to reach `[S]`, which is worth being honest about: this channel serves Ommelo, not the personal brand. Those are different goals and this is the one place they diverge.

## Tone

Plain and specific. Enthusiasm is allowed here in a way it is not on the other three, because a launch without any is odd, but it has to be about the problem rather than about the product. `[G]`

Never salesy. Never the launch-day voice that describes a small tool as a revolution.

## Machine-writing tells to avoid

- "Excited to share", "thrilled to announce", "after months of hard work".
- The problem-agitate-solve structure. Everyone on Product Hunt can see it.
- Describing the product with three adjectives before saying what it does.
- "Built for [audience] who want to [outcome] without [pain]." A template, and read as one.
- Fake origin stories. "It started when I noticed..." when it did not.
- Claiming a category. "The first AI-native design tool."
- Exclamation marks in every line.
- Any metric that has not happened.

## Usual shape

**For a launch post** `[G]`:

1. What it does, in one sentence, concretely enough that someone could repeat it.
2. The specific problem it came from, from Alif's own work. This is the part that only he can write and it is the part that makes a launch land.
3. What it does not do. Naming a limitation buys more credibility on this platform than any feature claim.
4. What he wants feedback on. A real question, not "let me know what you think".

Short. Six to twelve sentences. Launch posts that scroll are not read.

**For a comment on someone else's launch:** two to four sentences. A specific reaction to a specific part of what they built, from the position of someone who has built something adjacent. Not "congrats on the launch".

## Formatting

- Short paragraphs. Minimal formatting.
- Bullets acceptable for what it does and does not do.
- No hashtags.
- Images and a demo video matter here more than on any other channel. A launch without a visual underperforms regardless of the copy. `[G]`

## Punctuation habits

- **No em dashes.** `[S]`
- Full stops. Plain sentences.
- Exclamation marks: at most one, in a reply to a person, never in the launch copy itself.

## Emoji

`[G]` Product Hunt culture uses them heavily. Alif should not, beyond at most one in a reply. Adopting the local dialect he does not use is the same tourist problem as with X slang, and the restraint reads as confidence here.

## Named post formats

Only two of the seven apply:

**The Founder Note.** The launch itself, and any follow-up about what happened after. The unglamorous middle, still no metrics.

**The Small Artifact.** A specific thing built or changed, shown plainly. Works as a comment on someone else's launch when he has solved the same problem differently.

The other five do not belong on Product Hunt.

## Subjects

Effectively one: **Building Ommelo.** Occasionally **Design and AI in practice** when commenting on someone else's launch in that space.

Enterprise UX reality and Design leadership do not belong here.

## Words he would never use

Everything from the LinkedIn list, plus the launch-day vocabulary: "revolutionary", "game-changing", "disrupting", "10x your workflow", "the future of design", "supercharge", "effortlessly", "in seconds", "no more [pain]".

## Do

- Say what it does before why it matters.
- Name a limitation out loud.
- Ask for feedback on one specific decision.
- Reply to every comment on a launch, personally, the same day.
- Comment on other people's launches in the months before your own. Arriving only to launch is visible.

## Do not

- Do not use an em dash.
- Do not launch Ommelo without Alif's explicit go-ahead on the exact copy. This is a one-shot, public, hard-to-reverse action and it does not happen on a draft.
- Do not invent a user count, a waitlist number, a revenue figure or a testimonial.
- Do not claim a category or a first.
- Do not queue Product Hunt content in the daily run. This channel is event-driven.
- Do not ask for upvotes anywhere, on any platform. It violates Product Hunt's rules and it is obvious.

## Comment voice

The main activity on this channel in ordinary weeks.

Two to four sentences on a specific part of what they built. From the position of someone who has hit the same problem from a different angle: enterprise constraints, consultancy delivery, whatever is actually true.

Never "congrats". Never a comparison to your own product. Never a link.

## Message voice

**Now evidence-backed.** His three real sent messages: `[Q]`

> "Sure let me know, we can meet another day"
> "Hmm, apply and if you see any other role i can refer you"
> "Pls check your email"

One line, no closing full stop, no greeting or sign-off, "Pls" contracted, lowercase "i" left alone.

Rare on this platform. If a maker follows up after a comment, keep it to that one line and stay on the thing they built. Do not convert it into a pitch for Ommelo.
