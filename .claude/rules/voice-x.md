## Machine-writing tells to avoid

Everything in `voice-linkedin-alif.md` applies, plus these, which are specific to X:

- The one-line post that is just an aphorism with no referent. "Good design is invisible." Nothing anchors it and it reads as a quote graphic.
- Numbered threads where each tweet is a heading with no content.
- "A thread 🧵"
- "Most designers get this wrong."
- Opening with "Unpopular opinion:" or "Hot take:". If it were unpopular you would not need to announce it.
- Ending a post with a hook for the next one.
- The fake-vulnerable confession that resolves into a flex.

## Usual post shape

`[G]`

One observation per post. Load-bearing sentence first. If a second sentence exists it complicates the first, it does not restate it.

Max 220 characters. `[S — stated in the existing writer prompt]` That ceiling is well under the platform limit on purpose: it keeps posts quotable and screenshot-shaped, and it forces the cut.

Threads are allowed, but only for genuinely sequential material, and each post must stand alone if it is the only one someone sees. Default to not threading. A thread that should have been one post is the most common failure on this platform.

## Formatting

- No line breaks inside a single post at this length. If it needs structure it is too long.
- No bold, no formatting of any kind. X has none.
- No hashtags. They do nothing on X now and read as 2015.
- Links suppress reach. If a link is necessary, put it in a reply to your own post, not the post.

## Named post formats

Same seven names as LinkedIn, so the Notion Format column stays consistent, but they behave differently here:

**1. The Room.** One line of what was said, one line of what it meant. The quote is the post.

**2. The Correction.** "I used to think X. I was wrong because Y." Two sentences. This format is at its best on X, where the compression removes the temptation to soften it.

**3. The Quiet Cost.** One sentence naming a decision, one naming where the bill arrives.

**4. The Question I Keep Getting.** Question in the first line, answer in the second. No preamble.

**5. The Disagreement.** The claim, then the counter. Requires more care here than anywhere, because there is no room to steelman and it can read as cheap. Use sparingly.

**6. The Small Artifact.** A screenshot with one line of context. Images do carry well on X.

**7. The Founder Note.** Building Ommelo. On X this works better than on LinkedIn because the build-in-public audience is here. Still no metrics and no launch talk.

## Do

- Cut until it breaks, then add back one word.
- Post the observation, not the setup for the observation.
- Reply to people properly. On X, replies build more than posts do at low follower counts.
- Let a good post be the only thing you post that day.

## Do not

- Do not use an em dash.
- Do not thread when one post would do.
- Do not repost the LinkedIn body with the paragraphs removed. Different platform, different post, written separately.
- Do not put a link in the post body.
- Do not subtweet anyone, ever, including people who deserve it.
- Do not announce that you are about to say something bold.

## Comment voice

On X, replies are the main instrument, not a secondary one. `[G]`

One or two sentences. Open from your own position: what this looks like from inside a consultancy, from inside enterprise, from Dubai. Never open with an assessment of their post.

Do not restate their point. Do not reframe it as a lesson. Stay on their exact subject and speak from a different seat.

No links. No mention of Ommelo unless they asked what he is building.

Disagree with claims, never with people.
