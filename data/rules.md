# Privacy rails

## Status of this file

**Provisional, and this is the file where that matters most.**

The Kickstart asks for these rails in Alif's own words, from round five of the interview. Round five has not happened. So what follows is a conservative default drawn from his situation: employed at a large consultancy, working on named client accounts, and building a product on the side.

Conservative means: where a real interview would have found the line, this file guesses at a line further back. That is the right direction to err, but it means some of these will be tighter than Alif actually wants. **Loosening a rail is his call and nobody else's.** Tightening one is anyone's.

Replace this file wholesale in the interview session, using his sentences.

---

## People

- No named colleagues. Not managers, not reports, not peers, not the good ones.
- No one who has not agreed, in words, to be written about.
- No identifying detail that functions as a name. "My skip-level" and "the other senior on the account" identify a person to anyone inside the building.
- No stories where someone comes off badly, even anonymised. Especially anonymised. People recognise themselves and so do their colleagues.
- Family is off limits entirely.
- No screenshots containing anyone else's name, avatar or handle.

## Employer and clients

- IBM iX can be named as where he works. `[S — he states it publicly himself]`
- Client work is described by **shape and problem, never by account**. "A large automotive client" is fine. Toyota, Lexus, Al Ghurair and Nestlé are named in this repo's internal context and in a CV; they do not go in a post attached to a story about something going wrong.
- No screenshots of client work. Not blurred, not redacted, not "from an early version". `[G — but treat as hard until Alif says otherwise]`
- Nothing that is under NDA, and when unsure whether something is, assume it is.
- No internal process, tooling or org detail that is not already public.
- Nothing about a pitch, a bid or an account that has not been announced.
- He does not speak for IBM iX. Everything is his own view, and posts should not be phrased in a way that reads as company position.

## Money

- No salary, his or anyone's.
- No day rates, project values or contract sizes.
- No Ommelo revenue, funding, valuation or burn.
- No user or waitlist numbers unless Alif has decided to make that number public, deliberately, and says so.
- Nothing about pricing under consideration.

## Anything under negotiation

- No job conversations, his or anyone else's.
- No promotion, review or internal role discussion.
- No partnership, client or acquisition talk before it is announced.
- No unreleased product plans for Ommelo beyond what he has already said publicly.

## Claims

- Never invent a number, a result, a metric or an outcome.
- Never invent a user, a client quote or a testimonial.
- No experience Alif has not actually had. If a draft needs a story he does not have, the draft is wrong, not the story.
- No implied credential. He is a senior designer and a founder; he is not a head of design, a speaker, or an advisor unless he actually is.
- If a claim cannot be backed up when someone in the comments asks for the detail, it does not go in.

## Tone rails

- No punching down. Not at juniors, not at clients, not at other designers' portfolios.
- No subtweets and no vaguebooking about a situation at work.
- Disagree with claims, never with named people.
- Nothing posted while angry about something that happened that day. It keeps until tomorrow, and if it is still true tomorrow it is a better post.

## The screenshot rule

Before any image goes out: check the tabs, the sidebar, the notifications, the file names, the browser history strip, the calendar in the corner, and the reflection in any device. This is where confidential detail actually escapes, not in the copy.

## When unsure, ask. Do not post.

This is the whole file in one line. If a draft sits anywhere near one of these rails, the system asks Alif rather than making a judgement call. There is no version of this where guessing correctly is worth more than checking.
